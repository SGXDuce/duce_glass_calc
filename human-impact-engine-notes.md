## Purpose

- A new determination layer being designed to sit upstream of the existing wind/bite pathway engines, answering whether Grade A safety glass is required under AS 1288 Section 5 and which glass types/thicknesses are eligible — separate from the existing toggle, which only declares that the user has already made that determination themselves
- Sahil is deliberately more conservative than the standard on annealed glass usage in several places, and has decided most Human Impact aspects (leadlights, shopfronts, stairways, window seats, etc.) are out of scope for this phase
- The existing safety-glass toggle and its Section 14.7 table-per-pathway mapping (engineering-decisions notes) stay as the manual fallback; this engine is an optional questionnaire path that, once accepted by the user, overrides that mapping

## Architecture — the routes model

- Every rule that matches the user's answers contributes either a mandatory Grade A requirement or an alternative (annealed/heat-strengthened) compliance route; routes are tested against every other matching rule and only survive if they satisfy all of them — this is why annealed exceptions can't simply be merged into one result (e.g. "Grade A required, max area 1.2 m²" would be nonsense, since the area cap only applies to the annealed alternative, not to Grade A glass)
- Standing resolution rule (Sahil's own words, to be applied to any future clause conflict): when two clauses both apply to the same panel, the more stringent governs — using safety glass required, larger thickness, smaller area, or any combination
- Grade A requirement resolves as OR across matches; glass types resolve as intersection; minimum thickness takes the max; area/width caps take the min; any out-of-scope flag short-circuits the whole determination
- Table selection cannot resolve arithmetically (5.1/5.3/5.4 aren't "bigger or smaller"); locked-in precedence is **5.4 > 5.3 > 5.1** (5.4 is the bathroom table, most restrictive population — toughened family only, 6mm floor) — Sahil confirmed this and said he holds precedence authority on human impact calls himself, not Michael, since "this is beyond Michael's expertise"
- Annealed/heat-strengthened caps are resolved as a per-thickness search, not a flat min/max combination across all bands at once: for each candidate thickness, test every matching rule's own applicable band (the tier that thickness falls into) against the actual panel area/width; the thinnest thickness where every matching rule's own band accommodates the real panel wins. Two earlier-considered approaches were both wrong and corrected during the Phase 1 build — "treat all bands as simultaneous caps" (would wrongly reject a compliant 10mm/0.35m² door panel by applying the 3mm band's 0.10m² cap to it) and "pick best-fitting tier first" alone (right for one clause's own tiers, but doesn't handle multiple clauses each offering a different tier that must be satisfied simultaneously)
- Confirmed via the build: any matching clause requiring Grade A with no annealed alternative offered blocks the annealed route entirely, even when another matching clause on the same panel would have permitted a smaller annealed exception — e.g. a fully framed bathroom side panel (5.8 = Grade A, no exception at all) blocks annealed even though 5.3.1 alone would allow 5mm up to 0.30m²; both clauses apply simultaneously and 5.8 admits no escape
- Doors are entirely overridden by the bathroom rule when applicable (5.8 replaces 5.2, does not stack); side panels are not overridden — both 5.8 and 5.3.1 are checked and most-restrictive-wins resolves them, since Sahil confirmed both sections need to be checked for side panels specifically

## Opening type vs glazing method — the input model

- "What is the opening" (door / side panel / window) is a separate question from "what glazing method is used in it" — a door or side panel can contain an operable window; a window (or side panel) can be glazed as fixed panel/sash, louvre, or sashless
- Glazing methods per opening type: door → fixed panel/sash, louvre only (no sashless — a door leaf can't have the two-horizontal-edges-unframed condition). Side panel or window → fixed panel/sash, louvre, sashless
- If more than one method is selected for one opening, each method gets its own independent determination (its own geometry, its own result) — a door with a fixed section and a louvre section produces two separate results, not one merged result
- "Fixed panel" and "operable sash" were confirmed to carry identical Section 5 treatment (same rules, same output) — the only place they'd ever diverge is faceted/structural-silicone glazing, where only a fixed panel is possible. Sahil's resolution: don't build them as two methods — just rename the single "fixed panel" method to **"fixed panel/sash"** everywhere it appears. Deriving which one actually applies from the tool's pathway/angle inputs is explicitly parked for the future window-schedule rework, not solved here
- The 300mm-from-doorway-edge question is only asked for openings nominated as **window** (never door — a door can't be its own sidelight; never asked for side panel, since selecting that opening type already asserts the classification). A "yes" answer reclassifies the window internally as a side panel for rule-matching purposes only — the input still displays as "window", the reclassification is invisible in the UI and only shows up in the result's reasoning trail
- The 300mm reclassification and the resulting side-panel rule set only affects the **fixed panel/sash** method within a multi-method opening — a louvre or sashless section of the same opening is governed by its own clause (5.12 / 5.15) regardless of proximity to a doorway
- "Exposed edges" (needed to pick between Table 5.3's plain route and its 10mm-toughened-family route under 5.3.1(b)) is a new question, asked only for the fixed panel/sash method, only when the panel is classified as a side panel (directly or via reclassification) and framing is partly framed or unframed. It does not apply to louvre or sashless sections
- Faceted/structural-silicone glazing's framing condition (derived from angle in the existing pathway engine, not a raw user answer) is explicitly out of scope for this engine for now; when the future window-schedule feature shares one framing fact between the wind/bite engine and this engine, the same derivation will need an equivalent "is the joint a weatherseal (unexposed) or structural (needs its own exposed/unexposed read)" fact — flagged for later, not solved now

## Louvres (Clause 5.12) — corrections during build

- Louvres have no framing/support condition concept of their own for human-impact purposes — the geometry test for whether Grade A applies at all (bathroom/schools/aged-care/high-risk/mistaken-doorway) still runs using the opening's own dimensions, separate from the blade dimensions
- Clause 5.4 (mistaken for a doorway) **never** applies to louvres, full stop — not conditional on blade size. A louvre assembly (slats with gaps) structurally cannot present as a solid doorway/opening, so 5.4.1 was never a candidate for it in the first place; this superseded an earlier (wrong) build where 5.4 ran for louvres gated only by the blade≤230mm exception
- Clause 5.12 does not stand alone once Grade A applies — it only contributes the toughened-only type restriction and the blade-envelope gate (≤230mm wide, ≤1000mm long — beyond that, human impact is out of scope, specific design required, wind load still returned). The actual governing thickness still resolves via the same most-restrictive-wins mechanism as everywhere else: Sahil confirmed a bathroom's 6mm requirement overrides 5.12's own 5mm floor when both match the same louvre
- Note: Table 5.4 (bathroom lookup table, Clause 5.8) and Clause 5.4 (mistaken for a doorway) share a number by coincidence only — unrelated rules, confirmed no cross-contamination in the build

## Sashless (Clause 5.15)

- Two horizontal edges unframed; span = distance between the supported edges (side channels), confirmed by Sahil as the correct reading of Clause 1.4.51 for this purpose
- Fixed combinations only, each type independent: toughened 5mm to 1000mm span, toughened 6mm to 1200mm span, laminated 6mm to 750mm span — beyond a type's own highest listed span, that type is out of scope entirely (not extrapolated to a thicker option)
- Span sets a minimum thickness per type family; wind load can raise it further but never lower it (thicker-is-fine, same as everywhere else in the tool)
- Annealed and heat-strengthened are never available under 5.15 (Grade A required)
- Integration requirement for the real build (not yet in the mockup): sashless glass types must appear as checkboxes in the same convention as the rest of the AS 1288 tool (per Pathway 3's `buildP3GlassTypeCheckboxes()` pattern) — each checkbox carries whether it's selectable at all (out past its span ceiling) and, if selectable, the span-derived floor combines with wind load via `max()`; the user still picks which types to evaluate, the determination doesn't pre-select

## Height/distance trigger comparators — corrected

- "Within X mm" in the standard is inclusive: schools (1000mm), aged care (1500mm), bathroom (2000mm), and the side panel 300mm-of-doorway/1200mm-height triggers all use `<=`, not `<`
- Low-level residential's "less than 500mm" (Clause 5.5) is genuinely exclusive in the clause text and stays `<`
- Clause 5.4.2(c)'s "500mm or greater" exception is already inclusive in the other direction (`>=`) — unaffected by the above, just noted as already correct

## Full settled rule set

- **Doors** (5.2) — Grade A, Table 5.1, unless bathroom (5.8 overrides entirely). Annealed/heat-strengthened exceptions fully framed only: 3–4mm ≤0.1m²/125mm width; 5–8mm ≤0.26m²/300mm width (8mm inherits the 5/6mm band — Duce interpretation, not literal clause text); ≥10mm ≤0.5m² no width limit. Unframed doors: toughened/toughened laminated ≥10mm, no exceptions
- **Side panels** (5.3.1, within 300mm of a doorway edge, below 1200mm) — fully framed → Table 5.1 with 5mm annealed exception ≤0.3m²; partly framed/unframed with no exposed edges → Table 5.3 plain; with exposed edges → toughened/toughened laminated ≥10mm. Also checked against 5.5 (low-level) and 5.8 (bathroom) simultaneously when applicable, most-restrictive-wins
- **Mistaken for a doorway** (5.4, windows only, not doors/side panels/louvres) — Grade A, Table 5.1 framed/5.3 unframed, unless any one of: sight width ≤500mm, sight height ≤1000mm, sightline ≥500mm, opaque/patterned (leadlight deliberately dropped from this exception per Sahil's decision — narrower than the clause, safe direction), qualifying crash rail/transom (upper edge ≥700mm or bottom edge ≤1000mm), level difference ≥1000mm
- **Low-level residential** (5.5, sightline <500mm) — never applies to doors. Windows: fully framed only in scope (partly framed/unframed windows are out of scope — open AGWA question below); Grade A Table 5.1, with annealed/HS exception ≥5mm ≤1.2m² fully framed only. Side panels: checked alongside 5.3.1, which already covers every framing condition, so 5.5 rarely if ever changes the outcome for a side panel but is still evaluated
- **Bathrooms/ensuites/spa rooms** (5.8, below 2000mm FFL) — fully framed → Table 5.1. Partly framed/frameless → Table 5.4, toughened/toughened laminated, monolithic toughened 5mm if area ≤2.2m² (interim: monolithic toughened only, not laminated toughened, pending AGWA confirmation — see open questions), else 6mm. Vanity/bench exemption stated as a disclaimer note only, never calculated — wording pattern: state the requirement, name the exemption, say the tool doesn't assess it, point at the override
- **Schools/early childhood** (5.10.2, ≤1000mm, or any door) — Grade A, Table 5.1 fully framed/5.3 unframed
- **Aged care/nursing homes** (5.10.3, ≤1500mm, or any door) — same split as schools
- **Louvres** (5.12) — see dedicated section above
- **Sashless** (5.15) — see dedicated section above
- **High risk of breakage** (5.24) — user's own judgement, asked on every job regardless of building use (not narrowed to non-residential); Grade A Table 5.1/5.3 by framing; permanent barrier exemption stated as a disclaimer note only, matching the vanity pattern — user overrides if they judge the barrier removes the risk

## Accept / override flow

- Determination always ends with an explicit accept prompt, regardless of outcome (Grade A required, or no requirement found) — accepting sets the calculator's toggle and glass-type filter accordingly and records which mode produced the result
- Declining leaves the toggle under full manual control, all six types available, nothing applied
- Once accepted, breaking the toggle away from the determination requires an explicit warning in **both directions** (overriding a Grade A finding to declare no requirement, and overriding a no-requirement finding to declare Grade A required) — clears the questionnaire answers, reverts to manual mode, and is recorded on the report as an override
- Ineligible-by-grade types are greyed out/unselectable with the reason on hover; types eligible but failing a numeric cap (annealed over area, sashless over span) stay selectable and return a non-compliant result with the reason, consistent with how the rest of the tool already handles failed checks

## Out of scope (explicit disclaimer shown before the questionnaire starts)

- Not assessed: shopfronts (internal/external), stairway glazing, balustrades, mirrors and backed/adhered glass, wardrobe doors (mirrored or not), roller/tilting/sectional doors, shower screens/bath enclosures as assemblies, window seat glazing (Clause 5.13 — forms a backrest to a window seat, glass within 500mm of seat level; dropped entirely per Sahil's decision, no annealed alternative since its route was via the now-excluded Table 5.2), leadlights entirely (Clause 5.17 dropped; leadlight is not even used as a mistaken-doorway exception — narrowed to opaque/patterned only), faceted/structural-silicone glazing
- Situations the tool cannot judge: bathroom vanity/bench exemption, permanent barrier exemption, whether glazing is capable of being mistaken for a doorway with no listed exception met, operable windows in positions other than the one entered (Clause 5.14), louvre blades outside the tabulated envelope
- Rules not applied: Grade B safety glass anywhere (Sahil: Grade A only, tool-wide — even though 5.8 and 5.17 both permit Grade B in the standard), annealed exceptions on partly framed/unframed glazing except side panels' own 5.3.1 route, Table 5.2 (dropped entirely — no remaining reference to it once shopfronts/roller-doors/seating were cut), IGU area factors, marking/labelling, edge working, non-standard thickness adjustment
- Two Duce interpretations beyond literal clause text, tagged as such rather than as AS 1288: monolithic heat-strengthened tracked identically to monolithic annealed for every annealed exception (defensible but an extension, not literal); 8mm annealed in doors inherits the 5–6mm band's caps

## Open AGWA questions (Sahil's committee contact)

- Does the Clause 5.8 5mm/≤2.2m² allowance extend to toughened laminated, or monolithic toughened only? (Current interim build: monolithic toughened only)
- For low-level residential glazing that is a **window** (not a side panel) and partly framed: does AS 1288 intend Table 5.1, Table 5.3, or is this genuinely outside Section 5's scope? 5.5 requires Grade A regardless of framing but only offers an annealed alternative when fully framed, and nothing names a table for the partly framed case directly. This item stays unresolved in the build pending the answer — sashless windows and side panels don't hit this gap, since 5.15 and 5.3.1 already have full answers regardless of framing; it only affects an ordinary fixed-panel/sash window that isn't reclassified as a side panel
